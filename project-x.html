Rizzio archive · The Proj(ect)-X years

RIZZIO: THE FORMATION
& RISE OF PROJ(ECT)-X

Proj(ect)-X did not appear fully formed in a single moment.
The surviving press record traces a group building its profile through independent promotion, specialist radio, print coverage, live work and its own label. This timeline follows the contemporary press release, with the Kiss FM broadcast, surviving magazine material adding detail and the notorious live concert at London's legendary famous venue 'The Marquee Club'

From the first press notices and the “ROOTS RAP” identity to radio freestyles, live appearances, releases. This is an archive-led account. Dates are given as precisely as the available material allows; where a source gives only a month or broad period, no more exact date is invented!

A CHRONOLOGICAL DOCUMENTARY · 1991—1994

THE FORMATION

So my older bro Stone, had a friend called Yinka, whom he introduced to me back in late '91, early'92. Met him down Charing Cross Road in the West End, and yeah, we clicked. He's a guitar player, and we all would sort of meet up and link each other, you know, once every so often, maybe once a month or every other month or whatever during that period between early '92 through to early '94. At that time, Infrared and I shared a flat on Upper Street in Islington from October '93 through to July '94, and during that period, Yinka and Stone would come over to the flat, and on one particular occasion on Saturday 7th May '94, we had an impromptu freestyling session! Obviously Yinka knew that I was doing some rap stuff, and he's heard a few of my tracks and an album or two during that period, and yeah, we had a good time, we had fun, and yeah, that was that.

Then later on in the year in Sept '94, Stone and I shared a flat together in Turnpike Lane, Wood Green, I was writing some new stuff for the "I'll Get Mine In Yo' Home" album, and I wrote a track called "Journey Through Da Lon-Town", which inspired, the 'Lon-Town' slang/nickname, because London has always been a really dry, boring way to say "London"!!!.

Journey Through Da Lon-Town

I remember a Paul McCartney track called "London Town" and Daryl Hall & John Oates track "London, Luck & Love" and they sounded so dull and grey...
So I thought, "What's a cool way of saying London?" And my Hip-Hip inspiration, Too $hort, used to call Oakland "Oaktown".
So I thought, let me call London 'Lon-Town'. And, you know, it kind of worked. So I called it "Journey Through Da Lon-Town", and them guys heard it and they thought, yeah, it was cool!
Stone and Yinka, who collaborated together doing other musical projects, decided to sort of say, "Let's do a rap group together?"

A CHRONOLOGICAL DOCUMENTARY · 1995—1997

Enter 1995

So we were just doing jamming, freestylin' and stuff and whatever you know, and Yinka and Stone had already put together some early tracks called "The Sound", We Get Hype" and "Ghetto Scary Tales" in early '95 by themselves.

The Sound
We Get Hype
Ghetto Scary Tales

Soon after they invited me down to Southwark, South London where Yinka was based and they suggested to do a rework of "Journey Through the Lon-Town", which was eventually called "This Goes Out To The Lon-Town"...And yeah, Proj(ect)-X was born!

This Goes Out To The Lon-Town

During this time we were thinking of branding for the group name (this was conceived by Yinka and Stone) and a name for our independent record label - and it was me who came up with "Notes 4 Notes" an exchange on musical notes for cash notes!!

So I officially became The Executive Producer and we pressed our first single "Bad Lyrics" featuring Flexi 'The General Flex' and Kandace.

Infra-Red officially joined just after in the summer of '95 after another demo session featuring 2 girls called Cynthia and Debbie - the track was called "Renegades" and the rest as they say was history!!

Renegades
MAY—JULY 1995

FIRST SIGNS OF THE MOVEMENT

A press release begins with a crew led by Yinka, Stone and Rizzio.

The Proj(ect)-X press release, headed “PROJEX — the story so far..”, looks back on 1995 as the year the crew made its first impact on the music scene. It describes a determined attempt to break through the difficulties of the Black British music industry, moving from relative obscurity towards critical attention.

During May, the group’s press release and promotional cassette were circulated among music-business contacts. The first named press response came from Touch Magazine, where Hip Hop journalist and DJ Paul Raspcallion reviewed the crew. The release quotes his reaction:

“I have to take my hat off to...this crew that have the basic ingredients to go far!”
Paul Raspcallion, Touch — quoted in the Proj(ect)-X press release

In July, Echoes gave Proj(ect)-X a half-page feature by Sonia Poulton, accompanied by an exclusive photograph by Jenny Baptiste. The interview described the group’s sound as an innovative “ROOTS RAP” style.

SEPTEMBER—NOVEMBER 1995

NOTES 4 NOTES and BAD LYRICS

The move from promotion to an independently released record.

The press release says the group established its own label, NOTES 4 NOTES, and prepared its debut single, BAD LYRICS b/w TINGS AND STUFF. It presents this as a self-driven operation: Proj(ect)-X was not waiting for the industry to provide the infrastructure.

Touch gave the single another positive review. The record also received two weeks of consistent airplay on Choice FM in London, selected by Jenny “The Lady of Soul” Francis in a back-to-back selection alongside Das EFX, Blahzay Blahzay and The Lost Boyz. The release additionally reports heavy rotation on Kiss FM, particularly on Max ’n’ Dave’s Rap Show.

Max 'N' Dave Show KISS 100FM

The single was available through selected London record shops. In the 1st November Kiss FM interview, Yinka named Black Market, Unity and Uptown; Stone added Wild Pitch, while Flexi mentioned Moody’s in North London.

Source distinction: the press release calls BAD LYRICS the group’s debut single. The November Touch review uses the phrase “second album”. Those are the respective source descriptions; this page does not silently reconcile the difference.

1st NOVEMBER 1995

Kiss FM — interview and live freestyle

Proj(ect)-X on Max ’n’ Dave’s Rap Show.

On 1 November 1995, Proj(ect)-X - Yinka, Stone, Rizzio, General Flex and DJ Def K appeared on Kiss FM’s Max ’n’ Dave’s Rap Show. Max LX introduced the group and played BAD LYRICS before the interview. The transcript gives a snapshot of the crew speaking for itself: record shops stocking the single, people involved and the next planned stop on the radio circuit.

Yinka made a point of acknowledging Kandace, who could not be present in the studio but appeared on the track: “she’s on the track there, and she needs her respect... we ain’t forgot about her.” It is a direct contemporary acknowledgement of her contribution.

The broadcast also included a collaborative freestyle involving Proj(ect)-X, Phoebe One and Force. Max described the combination as a first for that configuration, with no rehearsal; afterwards he praised it as “straight in live off the top of the dome”. The transcript identifies verses from Yinka, Stone and General Flex within the session, alongside the wider collaboration.

8th NOVEMBER 1995

Bristol Galaxy FM

The next appearance was discussed on Kiss FM a week earlier.

The Kiss FM transcript points forward to Bristol: Yinka tells the presenters that Proj(ect)-X should be there the following Wednesday for a Galaxy FM session, followed by a club PA. The press release subsequently confirms the Bristol Galaxy FM appearance, placing it on 8th November — one week after the Kiss FM broadcast.

This interview featured Yinka, Stone, Rizzio, Infra-red, Juliet and DJ Def K and a freestyle from Yinka, Stone and Rizzio.

After the radio visit, the group went on to Rocksbury’s nightclub in Bath. According to the press release, China Black had cancelled, creating the opportunity for Proj(ect)-X to make an impromptu PA. The release says the group won over the crowd.

DECEMBER 1995 — JANUARY 1996

More press, more stages

The December round-up records both print and live activity.

The press release says Proj(ect)-X appeared on Radio 1’s Friday-night Rap Show with Westwood, where BAD LYRICS was played. It also says a future guest appearance had been promised. The promise is not evidence that the later guest appearance actually took place, so it is recorded as an announced prospect rather than a completed event.

December brought an interview with The Downlow, a live date at Easton Community Centre in Bristol and an appearance at London’s Marquee Club. The release describes technical problems at the Marquee but says the group delivered a strong set and received a positive audience response.

Looking ahead, the press release names GHETTO STREET STUFF and DESPERATE TIMES, set for early 1996, followed by KNOW THE GAME and PLAYTIME, described as “coming soon”.

At this time Juliet had replaced Kandace as Proj(ect)-X's female backing vocalist, performing on the above studio recordings.

1996

Project Mentalities

The group’s identity and ambitions continue to appear in the music press and on the airwaves.

In the spring of '96 the continuous airplay on BBC Radio 1 with the championed proposed new second single "C Da Real"

C Da Real

During the early part of the year, numerous 'Heads' in the industry failed to deliver, fake promises and typical B.S. created hurdles that just were not manageable to circumvent, so inevitably despite the on-going positive exposure, it just wasn't happening...
This lead to Rizzio writing a track called "Cap'm's Log" for his on solo project, telling of this sorry tale of sorry people...

Cap'm's Log

Further archive includes further coverage in Blues & Soul and Echoes. The Blues & Soul item is dated 23 October 1996. On 9 November, Echoes published “Project Mentalities”, naming Stone (26), Yinka (23), Rizzio (22) and Infra-Red (24).

The Echoes feature describes the group discussing a four-track EP and records an ambition to put N17 on the map. This is a marker of how Proj(ect)-X was being presented at this stage: not simply as a single-release act, but as a collective with a stated local identity and further plans.

1996 was an extremely busy year for the crew, recording an album's worth of tracks, the line-up from here on was Yinka, Stone, Rizzio and Infra-red with Juliet on backing vocals. General Flex made an appearance on the track "Bangin'"

Bangin'

Rizzio was still writing and recording his own solo albums alongside, with songs penned in anticipation for proposed entries for the upcoming Proj(ect)-X projects.

He along with Stone and Yinka created the concept of the gambling card metaphor theme that ran throughout the tracks, and subsequently dominated Rizzio's own solo efforts.
He also sketched a proposed idea/concept for the album title and cover, which revealed the then (unofficial) track listing.
This is what was in Rizzio's head and was never agreed, discussed or imagined by anyone else in the group.

The 1996 press file also preserves the WANTED front and back covers, alongside artwork from the Project X track sequence.



MARCH 1997

The Americans Set The Foundation

Proj(ect)-X album title becomes the headline of the next press chapter.

The March 1997 Hip Hop Connection feature is the latest dated magazine item in this archive sequence. Its subject is The Americans Set The Foundation — the title associated with the Proj(ect)-X album and the culmination of the group’s recorded-era identity.

The surviving press materials trace a progression: independent promotion in 1995; the BAD LYRICS single and radio campaign; the move through Bristol and Bath; further live pa's, The Marquee Club and magazine activity; and, by 1997, a substantial Proj(ect)-X album identity carried into the specialist Hip Hop press.
